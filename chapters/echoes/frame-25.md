# The First Blank Page

**Written by Avery Noble**

My name is Eli Noble.

Noble is self-appointed.

I should explain that before somebody mistakes it for inheritance, paperwork, or a decision made with the permission of anyone sensible.

I did not grow up with the name. I did not discover it in an old village record. Sister Orin did not give it to me, although she has given me enough other things without asking first.

It belonged to Avery.

I decided it belonged to me too.

By the time I made that decision, Avery had saved my life more than once, argued with nearly every useful choice I made, and developed the exhausting habit of being right shortly before something terrible happened.

He was also my best friend.

That description had become too small.

No rule I knew said a person could appoint a brother for himself. No useful rule said he could not. Avery was in no condition to object. So I appointed him.

Then I took his name.

Everything after this page had already happened by the time I sat down to make one account of it.

Most of it began earlier, on damaged slates, loose pages, route margins, the backs of supply records, and scraps I wrote while waiting for pain to become useful information. Some entries were made the same night the events happened. Others came later, after I understood that surviving something and understanding it were separate kinds of work.

I have put those pieces in the closest order I could prove. That is not always the order in which I remembered them, and it is not always the order in which the roads insist they happened.

This is not prophecy.

It is reconstruction.

At the beginning, I knew very little.

I knew Avery had disappeared while pulling me out of a place that wanted to correct my existence.

I knew Pebble had shown me an old hand becoming a paw, five Wayhounds beneath a gray sky, and a moment I had witnessed years before without understanding it.

I knew Avery was still inside him somehow.

Not alone.

Not whole.

Still Avery.

I did not know how to separate a person from a Wayhound without destroying either of them. I did not know where the between-place had kept the rest of Avery, whether his human body still existed, or what reaching him would demand from mine.

I did not know whether Meridian had a rule for any of it.

It did not.

We added several.

The first scrap I kept contained no route, no equipment list, and no credible plan. It contained Avery's name, written three times because once had not made the situation feel real.

Underneath it, I wrote one promise.

I would bring him home.

What follows is built from the things I wrote while trying to keep that promise and from what I could finally understand after it was kept. By the time I arranged these pages, the searching was over. The account was not.

Some pieces remain wrong around the edges. Some belong to roads that remember events differently than people do. I have marked uncertainty where I found it.

Where I could not mark it, I have told the truth as closely as it allowed me.

This is where Avery's account ends.

Mine begins with the promise to bring him home.

This is the story of how I got my brother back.
