# Cost of Return

Rook ran until the corridor stopped being a corridor and became a set of refusals.

Elijah's blood ran hot through the back of his coat. Not steadily. In pulses. That was the part Rook hated most. Steady blood could be lied about for a few more breaths. Pulses counted down.

Eli was ahead of him, moving too fast for a man who had just been slammed into a wall and too carefully for a man who trusted what he saw.

"Right," Eli said.

Rook took right.

"Low," Eli called.

Rook ducked under a brace split wide enough to take skin off his shoulder and felt Elijah make a sound against his back that was almost language.

"Step over the cut," Eli said.

Rook stepped.

The Braceback hit the narrow break behind them and made the stone answer with a deep, angry thud. Pebble streaked across the corridor behind it, pale fur catching seam-light in one brief slash, then vanished into a side shadow before the beast could decide whether killing him was worth rearranging itself.

"Not there," Eli said.

Rook did not take the left branch. A second later the left branch folded inward with a dry, patient grind.

"Good call," Rook said.

Eli did not look back. "Try sounding less surprised."

Rook adjusted Elijah's weight. "I'm trying several things."

Eli cut across a sloped brace. "Run better."

Rook cleared the brace by inches. "Carrying your almost-twin is affecting my elegance."

Elijah's hand tightened weakly in Rook's coat. "I object to almost."

"Noted," Rook said.

The fact that Elijah could still object felt indecently precious. Rook kept moving.

Eli cut them through a side break where the wall narrowed enough to make the Braceback's mass a problem rather than a privilege. It hit the pinch behind them, snarled, and lost a handful of seconds trying to force a body built for impact through a space built by someone with a grudge against large animals.

Pebble appeared ahead, spun once, and barked.

Eli followed him without ceremony.

The route dropped under their feet. Not stairs. The remains of stairs, which was worse, because it suggested the place had once cared about knees and then lost interest. Rook took the descent with Elijah's weight dragging one shoulder lower, blood making his grip slick where he had hooked an arm across Elijah's thighs.

"Rook," Elijah said.

Too quiet.

Rook kept his eyes on Eli's back. "Still here."

Elijah's answer was barely more than breath. "Good."

Rook did not answer that. He needed the breath.

Eli heard the voices first and changed direction before the sound reached Rook cleanly. His hand went out to the wall, fingers dragging over old chalk and newer dust, and his face tightened.

The corridor turned.

Light changed.

Voices ahead.

Sarah came around the bend fast enough to count as collision if Avery had not caught her by the arm a half second beforehand.

What she saw was not a reunion.

It was Rook carrying Elijah under enough blood to make every other fact about the scene secondary.

"Down," Sarah said at once. "Now."

Rook obeyed without question.

The moment Elijah's weight shifted off him, the man folded badly toward the wall, consciousness hanging on from what looked like principle more than physiology.

Sarah was already on her knees.

"Pressure kit," Sarah snapped.

Dr. Avery Chen came around the opposite side of the split route a heartbeat later, scanner still in hand. For one split second everything hit at once: the same face twice, the same startled pause, the same impossible resemblance trying to matter in the middle of blood loss.

Then Sarah looked up once and cut through both of them with, "Later."

That ended it.

Dr. Avery handed over gauze and seal-wrap from his field kit. Avery knelt opposite Sarah without waiting to be asked and got both hands under Elijah's ruined leg in the exact angle required to keep the wound open enough to work and closed enough not to worsen it. Rook saw Sarah notice that, catalog it, and move on.

Rook backed up one pace and discovered the wall behind him by hitting it with his shoulder. He had Elijah's blood down both arms. He had no useful amount of breath. The Braceback had stopped pursuing, or the route had decided the animal had made its point. He did not trust either possibility.

Eli stayed farther back than the rest of them. Pebble pressed against his leg hard enough to shove him off balance. Eli's eyes were on Elijah, but his body was turned slightly away, as if the pressure between counterparts had a physical edge he could not cross without cutting both of them.

Elijah's eyes opened once and found him.

For a beat the resemblance between them landed again, now with blood and exhaustion distorting all the clean lines. Dr. Avery saw it this time. So did Sarah.

No one said anything.

Sarah cut the trouser leg. She saw the wound and went still only long enough for terror to become process.

"He's lost too much," Sarah said. "Not all of it from one source. Good. Very good. That means we're still having a conversation."

Elijah made a weak protesting sound. "I was under the impression-"

"Quiet."

He obeyed.

Avery did not flinch under the pressure required to hold the leg steady. His face had gone flat with the kind of concentration Sarah trusted more than comfort.

Dr. Avery crouched near Elijah's head, checking pupils, breathing, coherence, because no decent emergency ever arrived alone.

"Stay with me," Dr. Avery said.

Elijah looked at him, then blinked as if the face in front of him had chosen a particularly unhelpful time to become complicated.

"There's... an unfair amount of you now," he murmured.

Dr. Avery might have laughed in another life.

"You can complain about it later."

Sarah sealed the dressing, bound it tight, then pressed again to test what reality had left them. Less blood now. Not none. But less.

She let out one careful breath.

"He's not dying," Sarah said.

The sentence hit the corridor harder than any shouted order had.

Not dying.

Not safe.

Not well.

Not remotely stable.

But alive enough for hope to risk being admitted into the room.

Rook slid down the wall before his legs turned the decision into an insult. Eli sat opposite, slower, one hand still braced on Pebble's rough neck. For a few seconds the group had something close enough to relief that no one risked naming it.

The quiet lasted until the comm hissed.

Kaden's voice broke through the comm. "Rook."

Kaden sounded as if the distance between worlds had begun charging interest.

Rook had the receiver up in an instant. "Here."

Kaden's question thinned under static. "Everyone together?"

Sarah answered before Rook could. "Enough of us. Elijah's alive."

Relief moved through the line as a silence, not a sound.

"Good," Kaden said.

That single word cost too much.

Sarah looked up. Dr. Avery stilled. Eli lifted his head as if hearing something under the voice that did not belong to ordinary exhaustion.

Rook knew before the next sentence arrived that it would matter.

"I don't think," Kaden said, and stopped long enough for static to crawl over the gap, "I don't think I feel well enough to keep-"

The line broke.

Not into silence. Into collapse. Static burst once, sharp and total, then vanished so completely that the absence sounded engineered.

Sarah hit her comm. "Kaden?"

Nothing.

Sarah tried again. "Kaden." Her voice did not rise. It became more dangerous.

Nothing answered.

Rook was already standing.

"He told me earlier he was hurting," Rook said.

Every eye turned toward him.

Rook gave them only what belonged to the crisis. "Holding the connection together was costing him more than he let on."

Sarah looked at the tablet in her hand, then at Avery's survey brace, then at the old marker stakes set into the worked wall around them. Her expression changed from fear into structure.

"We don't need comms," Sarah said.

Dr. Avery frowned. "What?"

Sarah turned to Avery. "We're already together. Your people use local anchors."

Avery understood before she finished. "To keep a route agreeing with itself."

Sarah dropped to one knee, already pulling a lead free from the tablet. "And ours uses identity lock to force a return-state through noise. Kaden still reads as one signal. Singular. If we stop trying to talk to him and find the signal..."

Avery unclipped a narrow survey brace from his belt. "Your device follows a body. This follows where a place is willing to admit a body passed through."

Dr. Avery looked from one tool to the other. "Those are not the same thing."

Sarah shook her head once. "No. They're the same problem."

Rook watched the realization pass through the chamber. Different worlds, different instruments, same shape underneath. Even half wrecked, even with Elijah bleeding on the stone and Kaden gone quiet, the discovery had a kind of terrible elegance.

"If this shorts my system," Dr. Avery said, "I would like it recorded that I objected."

"Object now," Sarah said. "Useful later."

He handed her a spare cell.

Avery laid the survey brace beside the tablet and pressed two fingers to the old metal. The lines etched into it brightened faintly, not with power exactly. With agreement. Sarah spliced the lead, and the screen tore itself sideways into white static.

For one second, nothing.

Then a single narrow spike punched through the noise.

Gone.

Back again.

West-southwest.

Lower.

Close enough to matter.

Unstable enough to lose.

"Again," Sarah said.

Avery adjusted the brace by fractions, turning it not toward geography but toward some local argument the route was making about where a person could still be found honestly. The spike returned, weaker but clearer.

"There."

Avery nodded once. "Lower shelf."

Rook exhaled. "That's him."

Sarah rose. "Then we chase it."

Elijah pushed himself up on one elbow and immediately regretted believing in elbows. "Please tell me this is the part where nobody recommends rest."

Sarah gave him a look sharp enough to sterilize tools. "You are not walking."

Elijah tried and failed to sit straighter. "That was not a yes."

Rook stepped forward. "I'll carry him."

Sarah looked at the blood still drying across Rook's coat.

Rook met Sarah's eyes. "Unless you have a better proposal."

Sarah glanced toward the route. "I have several better proposals. None of them are available."

Dr. Avery helped secure Elijah for movement. Rook took the weight more carefully this time, lower and closer, using the wall when the corridor narrowed. Elijah's face went gray but he did not black out.

Elijah spoke through his teeth. "That is encouraging. In the least encouraging way possible."

Rook shifted him once. "If you vomit on me, I will consider it a betrayal."

Elijah closed his eyes. "Noted."

The tablet pulsed again.

There.

Then nowhere.

Sarah took lead with local Avery through the upper cut. Rook carried Elijah behind them. Dr. Avery stayed close enough to monitor the wound and far enough not to trip them both. Eli moved near the center with Pebble tight against his leg.

At first no one named what was wrong with Eli.

The corridor did it for them.

He stopped at a split and stared down a clean-looking branch with too much attention. His hand went to his hip, found nothing, and curled once in the empty air.

Avery saw the movement.

He stopped so sharply Sarah nearly walked into him.

"Where is it?" Avery asked.

Eli blinked. "What?"

Avery's gaze dropped to Eli's empty belt. "Your flask."

Silence fell badly.

Eli's hand searched the empty place again. "I had it," Eli said.

Rook looked over Elijah's shoulder. "Before the Braceback?"

Eli shook his head, then stopped halfway through the motion as if the corridor had tilted under him. "During. Before. I don't-" He closed his eyes for one hard beat. "I lost it."

Avery's face did not change, which was how Rook knew it was bad.

Avery did not soften. "You need it," Avery said.

Eli's answer came with tired fury. "Yes. I noticed the part where it's gone."

"No," Avery said. "You noticed the object is gone. I am saying the rest of us need to notice what that means."

Eli's mouth pulled tight. "Thank you for the public confidence."

"You want me lying?" Avery asked.

Eli's answer came flat. "No."

Avery held his gaze. "Good."

The word landed harder than comfort would have. Sarah watched Eli absorb it like another injury he did not have time to resent properly.

Avery looked at the group. "Without it he goes wrong," Avery said. "Not evil. Not useless. Wrong. His head stops agreeing with what is there."

Pebble gave a low, unhappy sound and leaned harder into Eli.

Sarah looked directly at him. "Can you still tell me when you stop trusting what you're seeing?"

Eli met her eyes.

For the first time since Sarah had seen him, the man looked very young.

"If I know first," Eli said.

Rook snorted once. "Comforting standard."

That got the smallest twitch from Eli's mouth.

"You say that," Eli said, "like you've ever once aimed higher."

"I had a whole speech prepared about your many admirable habits," Rook said. "Losing the flask has made it impossible to deliver honestly."

Eli rubbed one hand over Pebble's skull. "Terrible timing on my part."

"Recurring issue with you," Rook said.

The exchange was brief enough to miss if someone had been waiting for sentiment. Avery did not miss it. Neither did Sarah.

She looked back down at the tablet. The spike returned, closer now.

Sarah touched the tablet again. Another spike. Lower now. Closer.

"Move," she said.

They did.

No one argued.

Not even Eli.

That frightened Rook more than the blood.

They found Kaden in a chamber that had once believed in order.

That belief had not held.

The shelf was cut into a larger worked space supported by split arches and brace-columns whose metal hearts showed through where stone had worried at them for too many years. Marker stakes leaned at angles that suggested prior geometry. One entire side had sheared inward, leaving broken floor sloping toward a lower cut where pale seam-light breathed through cracks.

Pebble reached the edge first and barked once.

Sarah dropped beside him and forced the tablet flat against the stone.

The signal did not spike this time.

It bloomed.

Sarah's voice tightened. "He's here."

Rook moved before she finished. Avery caught his sleeve and redirected him around the unstable edge, then went down first with the ugly confidence of a man reading support failure faster than anyone else in the room.

Kaden lay half under a collapsed crossbrace, one arm flung above his head as if his body had stopped negotiating on the way down. His comm rig was still clipped to his shoulder, dark except for one weak pulse under dust. Blood marked one ear. More at the corner of his mouth.

Alive.

Not safely.

Alive.

Sarah reached him at the same time Avery did.

"Don't move the brace yet," Sarah said.

"Wasn't planning to," Avery answered.

Dr. Avery crouched opposite, scanner in hand. Rook knelt at Kaden's side too fast to pretend procedure had brought him there.

Rook's hand stopped short of Kaden's face, then redirected to his shoulder. "Kaden. Kaden."

No answer.

Sarah checked airway, pulse, pupils. Alive. Barely responsive. Breathing shallow but regular enough not to terrify her instantly into the worst branch of medicine.

"Concussed," Dr. Avery said. "And stressed to hell."

"Useful field classification," Sarah said.

Dr. Avery did not look up from the scanner. "I contain multitudes."

Rook did not look away from Kaden. "Can he move?"

Sarah glanced at the pinning brace. Bent metal. Broken stone. Bad angle.

"With care," Sarah said. "Which means not like that."

Rook's hand had already closed around the brace.

He let go.

Rook released the brace. "Cruel woman."

Sarah and Avery freed Kaden together. Not fast. Fast belonged to clean rooms and good architecture. Avery took the stone weight in both hands while Sarah turned the twisted metal just enough for Rook to slide Kaden clear without wrenching what still belonged inside him.

The moment pressure came off, Kaden made a sound through his teeth.

Not awake.

Not absent.

Rook went still around him.

"Easy," Rook said, far too quietly for someone unconscious. "Don't embarrass me by dying in a ruin."

Kaden's lashes moved once.

Sarah saw the blood on the comm rig and understood one piece of it. "He kept forcing the line open."

Rook did not answer.

He did not need to.

Eli had come halfway down the slope and stopped there, one hand against the wall, the other pressed hard to the side of his head. A thin line of blood had started from his nose. He wiped it away with irritation rather than drama.

"Do not come down the left side," Avery said without looking up. "It's lying."

Eli swallowed. "I know."

Sarah looked up sharply. "What?"

Eli's eyes tracked to a stretch of stable-looking stone that had not moved once since they entered the chamber.

Eli's gaze stayed fixed on the false-safe stone. "It isn't stable," he said. "It only wants us to think it is."

Pebble barked once, low and urgent.

Avery took Eli's wrist. "How bad?"

"Annoying," Eli said.

"Meaning bad," Avery said.

"Meaning if I answer honestly you'll tell me to sit down somewhere while all of you carry a man and a portal problem without me."

Avery let go of his wrist. "If I thought sitting you down would solve this, I would have done it already."

That silenced Eli for half a second.

Then Eli laughed once through the pain. "Cruel man."

"Recurring issue with me," Avery said.

Rook looked up, catching the echo of his own line, and for one second the group had shape again. Not peace. Not safety. Only the remembered outline of one.

Sarah checked Kaden again, then looked at Eli. "Can you walk straight?"

Eli answered without decoration. "No."

The honesty was immediate enough that she trusted it more than reassurance.

"Can you tell me when you're wrong?"

He took too long to answer.

"Sometimes," Eli said.

"Fine. Then no one takes a route call from Eli alone. If he sees something, Avery confirms it or Pebble does. Understood?"

Eli nodded once.

They moved out slower than before and therefore more dangerously.

Kaden had to be carried. Elijah had to be half-carried, half-walked, his leg bound tight and his face drained of color. The tablet had to be protected and used. Every few minutes, the route reminded them that the Continuum did not consider basic anatomy persuasive.

Sarah took lead with Avery through the upper cuts. Rook carried Kaden because no one else challenged him fast enough and because Kaden's head settled against his shoulder in a way that made refusal feel impossible. Dr. Avery stayed with injured Elijah. Eli moved behind with Pebble because no one could fully trust his route calls alone.

"How close?" Dr. Avery asked once.

Avery touched the wall, listening with the backs of two fingers.

"To the portal?" Avery said. "Too close for confidence. Close enough for consequences."

"Poetry from you is medically concerning," Elijah said.

He sounded gray.

Rook looked back over one shoulder. "You'll forgive us if the atmosphere is not clinic grade."

Kaden woke in fragments.

First a jerk against Rook's grip. Then one hand flexing. Then a rough breath as if he had surfaced in the wrong century and objected on principle.

Rook shifted him down enough to see his face. "Kaden."

Kaden's eyes opened.

Confusion moved through them first. Recognition followed. Then something older and quieter than panic.

"You all look terrible," Kaden whispered.

"Leadership failure," Rook said.

Kaden's mouth moved by fractions. "You should have delegated."

The laugh that passed through the group was brief, ugly, and useful.

Sarah crouched long enough to check him. "Stay with me. Can you track where you hurt?"

"Can I?" Kaden asked. His voice was dry enough to abrade the air. "Or would you like the highlights."

Sarah kept checking Kaden's pupils. "Highlights."

Kaden's mouth barely moved. "Head. Chest. Teeth. Pride."

"Normal, then," Rook said.

Kaden looked at him more clearly.

Something in that look made Eli turn away.

"You found me," Kaden said.

Rook tightened his hold before Kaden could slip. "Still trying not to make a hobby of it."

The corridor ahead narrowed.

Avery's posture changed first.

Portal region.

Sarah knew it before Avery said anything because the tablet began misbehaving in a new dialect. Not static this time. Doubling. Return ghosts on the screen. The same route trying to become several nearly identical lies.

"We're close," Sarah said.

Avery's eyes stayed on the route. "Yes."

Eli stopped.

Rook turned immediately. "What."

Eli stared ahead and to the right. Not at the route Avery had just checked. Not at Pebble, who had gone rigid on the left side of the corridor with his fur lifted and mouth shut.

Eli stared toward the wrong side of the corridor. "Did you hear that?"

No one answered at first because they had all heard something.

Not the same thing.

For Sarah it was a crackle in the stone.

For Dr. Avery, a shift in corridor support.

For Elijah, judging by his expression, the sudden tightening in Eli's face.

For Pebble, it was enough to make him lunge two steps left and bark once, sharp and furious.

Eli looked right.

That was the error.

He flinched toward the wrong side of the corridor with the raw certainty of a man answering danger he believed had already reached them.

"Right side!" Eli snapped. "Move Kaden right!"

Pebble barked again from the left.

Avery shouted at the same instant, "No-"

But Rook had already committed.

There were people who hesitated under contradictory input and people who chose a line and spent themselves on it without reserve. Rook had never belonged to the first category.

He drove forward with Kaden's weight, turning his own body between the waking man and what Eli had called as threat. It was exactly the move Sarah would later understand any of them might have made for someone they loved enough or feared losing enough.

Except the danger was not on the right.

The right side only wanted them looking there.

The true failure came out of the left wall where the portal approach had been stitched too many times and trusted too often: a hidden limiter spar buried in the stone gave way under seam-pressure, tore loose with a shriek of old metal, and came across the corridor in a single violent sweep.

Pebble had known.

That was what he had been trying to tell them.

Kaden saw it in the same instant Sarah did.

His eyes went wide.

"Rook-"

Rook turned hard on the sound and made the last choice he had time to make.

Rook threw Kaden clear.

Not heroically.

Practically. Fast. The exact ugly angle needed to put the other man out of the path of the spar.

The metal missed Kaden by inches.

It caught Rook across the ribs and drove him backward into the broken brace-column behind him with enough force to make the corridor answer.

The sound was one Sarah had no professional interest in hearing again.

For a second the world became pieces.

Dust.

Stone.

Kaden hitting the ground shoulder-first and rolling badly.

Elijah shouting something from too far away.

Pebble's bark cutting short into a raw animal noise.

Eli frozen as if the wrongness of what had happened had exceeded even panic's ability to use him.

Then motion came back all at once.

Sarah reached Rook first with Avery half a step behind her.

He was alive.

That was the first and worst fact.

Alive enough to know.

Alive enough to hurt.

The spar had not pinned him through. It had struck, broken, driven, and torn. One side of his chest moved wrong. Blood was already spreading beneath him in a dark deliberate sheet. His breathing came shallow and fast, then shallower.

Sarah's hands were on him before thought finished arriving.

"Pressure," she said.

She did not know to whom.

Avery was already there. Dr. Avery dropped beside them. Kaden, half upright now, stared across the shattered feet of corridor with the look of someone watching a fact he had feared for years finally decide to become visible.

Eli had not moved.

Rook's eyes found him anyway.

That was the cruelest part.

Not that Eli had made the call. That he had to live long enough inside himself to see the result waiting for him in another man's face.

Sarah felt Avery register the same thing.

He did not look at Eli.

He looked only at the wound and said, with pure savage concentration, "Not his fault now. Work."

It was not absolution.

It was command.

Sarah obeyed it because her hands already were.

The wound was too large and moving wrong. Blood kept finding new places to go. Each breath hitched lower than the one before, as if his body had begun giving ground by inches it could not afford.

Sarah saw Dr. Avery understand it.

Avery understood it too, almost before either of them did, because men who lived in bad places learned the arithmetic of endings faster than medicine ever wanted admitted.

Rook understood it last only because shock delayed the courtesy.

"No," Kaden said from the floor.

It came out low and broken enough that Sarah almost would have preferred screaming.

Rook's mouth moved by fractions. "Helpful analysis."

The line should not have landed. It did. Because he was himself even there. Even then.

Kaden made a sound that folded in on itself somewhere between laugh and wreckage.

Eli came forward one step.

Then another.

There was blood on Eli's face now from the earlier nosebleed. More where he had wiped it with the back of one hand and failed to improve the result. He looked like a man approaching a verdict that had already been passed elsewhere.

"Rook," Eli said.

Just the name.

Rook looked at him. Not accusingly. Almost impatiently, as if Eli had missed something obvious and was wasting the remaining time arriving at it.

"You had one job," Rook said.

Sarah felt everyone in the corridor go rigid.

Then Rook took one painful breath and finished, "Stay where I can see you."

It was so dry, so nearly the shape of ordinary speech, that Eli's face broke around it.

"I told you right," Eli said.

"Yes," Rook answered.

No cruelty.

Only fact.

"Bad habit."

Eli made a wrecked sound and dropped to his knees hard enough to bruise.

"It's my fault this happened," Eli said.

The words tore out of him raw enough to sound involuntary.

"It's my fault this happened."

"I know," Rook said.

Rook, dying in a broken corridor with the portal close enough to smell at the edge of the air, chose not to waste what was left on ceremony.

He looked once toward Kaden instead.

Not long.

Long enough.

Kaden had dragged himself closer despite Sarah's total lack of permission. Blood from his split lip had dried at the corner of his mouth. His face had gone white in the way illness and grief negotiated when neither intended to concede.

Rook saw him and the smallest trace of something gentler crossed what pain had left of his expression.

"Still here?" Rook asked.

Kaden choked on the answer. "Yes."

"Annoying for you."

That finally got him. Kaden folded over the sound of a laugh he could not complete.

Sarah pressed harder, not because it would save Rook now, but because surrender felt too much like agreement.

Rook's eyes shifted once more, past them all, toward the dim glow west where the portal was holding because Sister Orin had not yet permitted reality another opinion.

"Move," he said.

Sarah shook her head before she knew she was doing it. "No."

Rook looked back at her with tired almost-amusement, as if he had found a scientist behaving emotionally in the field and chosen not to embarrass her for it.

"Doctor," he said, and it was impossible to tell whether he meant her or the other Avery.

Maybe both.

"He's right," Avery said quietly.

Sarah wanted to hate him for being correct at a moment like that. The effort failed for lack of time.

The corridor was still coming apart around them. Pebble had stationed himself at the broken left side and would not take his eyes off the dark beyond it. Elijah was trying and failing to stand without help. The portal region hummed through the stone with the sickening high-thin sound of maintained instability.

Rook did not have enough life left in him for argument and everybody knew it now.

That was when Sarah let herself feel it.

Not grief. Not yet.

Only defeat.

Small enough to carry.

"We move him," she said.

Dr. Avery's eyes met hers for one terrible second. He answered the question she had not wanted to say.

"We can't."

Rook exhaled once through his teeth. "Charming discussion."

Eli bowed forward until one hand caught him on the floor. Pebble left the corridor mouth at once and came to him, pressing hard against his side. Eli gripped handfuls of the Wayhound's rough fur like a drowning man selecting one fact he still believed.

Rook watched that too.

"Good dog," he said, and the compliment was somehow devastating in its ordinariness.

Kaden had reached him by then. He did not touch the wound. He knew enough not to do that. He only put one shaking hand against Rook's shoulder and kept it there as if contact could still serve any purpose not already denied.

Rook took one more breath.

Then another, shallower.

His fingers slackened under Kaden's hand.

The focus went out of his face by degrees, not all at once but enough that the man still lying there stopped being reachable by any ordinary route Sarah knew.

His chest rose once more.

Did not quite settle.

Then stopped trying to rise at all.

And because the Continuum was obscene in ways the real world had at least the decency to organize better, the portal gave a violent shudder in the same second, as if reminding the survivors that grief remained secondary to logistics unless they intended all of it to become meaningless at once.

No one said his name immediately.

Because saying it would have made it singular.

Made it finished.

Kaden did first.

Not loudly.

Not with theater.

Just once.

"Rook."

There was nothing in the corridor after that but the sound of people continuing to breathe because their own bodies had failed to receive better instructions.

Then Sister Orin's voice reached them through the comm in one harsh crackle of signal somehow forced back to life by proximity to the gate.

"If you intend to live," she said, "come now."

That ended the moment.

Not because it deserved ending.

Because bad worlds made that choice for you.

Kaden rose too.

Not cleanly.

Not wisely.

But under his own power, with one hand still on Rook's shoulder and the other braced against broken floor as if refusal alone could keep the world from finalizing around him.

"No," he said.

Sarah turned at once. "Kaden-"

"No." He looked at none of them. Only at Rook. "We're not leaving him here."

"I can't let him die here."

He bent as if to get both hands under Rook's weight and pull him up by force, by loyalty, by whatever was left in him that still believed effort could overrule fact.

Dr. Avery caught him first. "Don't."

Kaden tore free hard enough to nearly lose his footing.

"Get off me."

Avery crossed the space in two strides and got hold of his arm.

"Kaden."

"I said no." The words came out raw and half-shredded. "I'm not leaving him. Not him."

The corridor gave another long metal groan somewhere west. Dust fell from the ceiling in a dry slow veil.

Sarah saw the exact second Avery understood that persuasion had already failed.

He looked at Dr. Avery once.

The older man nodded.

Then both of them took Kaden at once.

Not gently.

Not cruelly either.

Just with the kind of practiced force people used when the only alternative was letting grief become another body on the floor.

Kaden fought them.

Badly, because he was injured and half-spent and half out of his mind with it.

But honestly.

He got one hand free and caught at Rook's sleeve. The fabric slipped through blood and dust and did not bring the man with it.

"Rook-"

"We are," Dr. Avery snapped back, still holding Kaden as the younger man tried once more to wrench free.

Not wild now.

Worse, in some ways. Clear.

Whatever in him had been seeing wrong a minute earlier had narrowed into one hard brutal line of usefulness. Grief had not steadied him. It had simply burned away everything not immediately real.

Kaden stared at him.

Eli's face was blood-streaked from the nosebleed, gray with pain, and emptied out to essentials.

"If you stay," Eli said, "then I get you killed too."

That landed.

Not because Kaden agreed.

Because some ruined surviving part of him still knew Eli was telling the truth.

His resistance broke by degrees rather than all at once. Not surrender. Structural failure.

Sarah stood first because someone had to. Avery took Elijah. Dr. Avery and Eli got Kaden between them and pulled him back while Rook's blood was still wet on all of them.

Pebble barked once.

"Good," Eli said at once. "Stay with me."

No one touched Rook again.

That would live in all of them later in different and specialized ways.

Sister Orin had stayed exactly where she promised.

The frame had spent the last several minutes trying to punish her for it.

The Anchor Maw Core burned white at the heart of Malrec's unfinished work. The Frame Jack had bitten so far into the brace stone that Orin's palm had gone numb from the handle. The portal surface bowed inward, then outward, trying to become a mouth and a mirror and a wound all at once.

It still did not have a limiter.

It still wanted one.

Orin could feel the absence with every tooth of the jack. Not theory. Not language. Hunger translated through old stone.

Kaden's voice had vanished from the relay, then returned as proximity and pain made the line easier and worse at the same time. Through the broken signal she had heard enough.

Rook.

Metal.

Kaden refusing.

Eli's voice, too empty for a living boy.

He's already gone.

Orin did not close her eyes.

The frame would have used even that.

She drove the jack down one more tooth and felt something tear in the skin of her palm.

Orin drove the jack down one more tooth. "Hold."

The stone held.

For now.

The survivors came out of the dark in pieces.

Sarah first, tablet clutched against her chest, face set in the blank hard shape of a person who had put grief in a container because there was not room for it yet. Dr. Avery came with Elijah half-supported against him. Avery was under Elijah's other side, local hands and real-world hands briefly sharing the same task without either man wasting the moment on the strangeness of it.

Kaden came between Eli and Dr. Avery for three steps, then almost fell. Eli caught him. Kaden tried to turn back. Eli held him upright with a grip that looked too harsh until Orin saw the way Kaden's knees had stopped negotiating.

Pebble came last, facing backward more often than forward, teeth bared at an empty corridor.

No Rook.

Orin had known before she saw it.

Knowing did not make the absence lighter.

"Through," Orin said.

Sarah looked at her once. The question in her face was not whether Orin could save him. Sarah already knew the answer to that. The question was whether the world was truly going to require them to obey.

Orin did not offer mercy she could not pay for. "Now."

The portal snapped bright around the edges.

The first pulse bent every chalk mark in the workshop toward it. The second lifted dust from the floor. The third made the relay split with a sound like a wire losing patience.

Sarah and Dr. Avery got Kaden to the threshold first.

Kaden fought the threshold when he understood. Not effectively. Enough to make his refusal real.

"No," Kaden said, voice torn down to almost nothing.

Eli leaned close enough for Kaden to hear and not enough to trigger the frame's interest. "Live and hate me later."

Kaden stared at him.

Then the portal took him.

Dr. Avery and Avery forced Elijah through second because medicine had not stopped being triage simply because grief had arrived. Elijah vanished in a burst of pale distortion.

Sarah went after them, because whatever waited on the other side would need someone upright enough to make the next decision. Dr. Avery followed somewhere around the same impossible beat, one hand near the frame until the last second as if the engineer in him still wanted a reading from the thing trying to kill them.

The threshold flared.

Not cleanly.

Not as a place.

Light without stone under it.

Air with the wrong weight.

Edges too smooth for the Continuum to believe in.

Only the local cast remained.

Avery.

Eli.

Pebble.

Orin.

And the corridor behind them, where Rook's body lay beyond sight.

He looked back once.

Not at the portal.

Not at Orin.

At the route where Rook was no longer visible.

Pebble barked once, furious and grieving.

Then Eli chose.

He did not announce it. He did not look to Avery for permission or to Sister Orin for instruction. One breath he was standing at the edge of the frame, and the next he had put one foot into the threshold after the vanished strangers, trying to follow them through by will alone.

The portal rejected him.

Not like a locked door.

Like a body refusing a wrong organ.

The light caught Eli at the shoulder first. His outline split into hard-edged copies that did not agree on where his arm belonged. His hand broke into pale facets, then snapped back with a sound too small for the damage it promised. For less than a heartbeat, Orin saw the frame trying to make him into the missing piece it still wanted: not traveler, not guest, not survivor.

Limiter.

Eli made no sound at first.

That was worse.

Then the threshold pulled again, and his breath tore loose.

Avery hit him from the side and got both arms around his waist. Pebble lunged low and clamped his teeth into the back of Eli's coat, bracing all four legs against the stone. The frame did not listen.

The nosebleed stopped while Orin was looking at it.

Not slowed.

Stopped.

The blood on Eli's upper lip dried in a thin black line. The angry bruising along his temple did not clear. It thinned at the edges and sank inward, purple-yellow under skin that had gone too smooth above it, as if something had corrected the surface and left the pain behind to explain itself.

Pebble planted himself across Eli's chest and snarled at the frame.

Orin's hand slipped on the Frame Jack.

The portal tried to use the mistake.

She recovered before it could.

Avery caught Eli under the arms and dragged him farther from the threshold. Eli came with him because some part of him had stopped making separate decisions.

Orin released the Frame Jack by one tooth.

The frame screamed.

Not sound. Pressure.

The workshop bowed inward around the unfinished door. Malrec's old chalk marks flashed in sequence, one clever mistake after another. The Anchor Maw Core flared so brightly the shadows vanished for half a breath.

Light folded inward. The center went thin. The edge snapped once, twice, and the final answering pulse threw all four of them back from the frame. Avery hit a workbench hard enough to break a corner. Eli went to one knee with Pebble pressed across him, the bad leg folding late and badly under him. Orin stayed upright only because one hand was still locked around the jack and pride had occasionally proved useful in emergencies.

Damaged stone.

Hot in the joints.

Hungry-looking in the wrong places.

Silent.

Eli stared at the dead frame. "No."

Too easily.

With pain arriving late.
