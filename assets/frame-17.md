# The Replacement

The frame took him, and then something else took the frame.

Rook felt himself being crowded out before he understood what was happening. His edges went wrong first—fingers breaking into shapes that weren't his, features shifting between the face he'd worn all his life and one he'd never seen. Memory flooded in where it shouldn't have, two lives demanding the same space, timelines colliding like badly thrown stones.

The pain was brief. The replacement was not.

Then he was fading, and someone else was standing where he had been.

—

The frame shivered.

The air in the workshop compressed, then released wrong.

Avery looked up from the diagram. The silence had weight, the kind that usually meant something had either finished or begun without permission.

Beside Eli's boot, one broken half of his staff clicked against the floor though no hand touched it.

Orin still had both hands locked around the frame jack. Her shoulders had gone rigid under the strain of keeping Malrec's damaged stone from deciding on a worse shape.

Avery turned.

The frame was empty.

Then it wasn't.

A man stepped through the threshold and into Malrec's workshop. He looked like Rook. He moved like Rook. But the pressure around him was wrong—too sharp, too clean, like something that had been measured to the decimal and filed down until nothing rough remained.

His equipment was wrong too. Not the worked cord and seasoned wood of a route-worker's gear, but something sleek and unfamiliar, materials that looked like they belonged in a place where nothing ever aged.

Avery's hand went to the frame where the Anchor Maw Core still rested in the central depression.

The man looked at them.

He looked at Avery.

He looked at Eli.

Then he looked at his own hands as if surprised to find them attached to him.

"Didn't you just enter that portal?" Eli said.

The words came out thin, the drag gone from his voice entirely.

Rook's brow furrowed. He touched his temple, then his chest, as if checking that he was still whole. When he spoke, his voice was Rook's voice, but the cadence was wrong—too precise, too measured, like someone who had learned to speak from manuals instead of roads.

"I'm..." Rook stopped. His eyes unfocused for a half-second, as if listening to something only he could hear.

He looked from Eli to Avery, and the wrongness in his face sharpened into recognition that did not belong to this room.

"You look… exactly like them. But you're not them, are you?"

"Our Rook went into that portal and never came back."

Avery made a careful mark on his slate. *Subject emerged claiming alternate origin. Hybrid characteristics noted. Portal behavior inconsistent.*

Rook felt the truth settle. "If your Rook disappeared into that portal… and I came out of it… then it is a bridge."

The bridge shuddered behind him as if it objected to being named.

"Kaden, can you hear me?" Rook's enhanced scanner sliced cleanly through the interference. "Your experimental comms might cut through this."

The reply arrived clear and immediate from the scanner speaker, loud enough for Avery, Eli, and Orin to hear. "Rook! Clear as day. The void is boosting your signal."

Kaden relayed without pause: "Dr. Sarah Chen, I have Rook. He has found a stable portal."

The line thinned while Kaden turned his attention elsewhere. Whatever answered him did not cross the bridge.

"Sarah wants to know if it is safe," Kaden relayed.

Rook made the decision. "Tell her I just went home and back. Watch this."

He stepped through the portal, vanished for three measured seconds, then returned through the same shimmering surface. The transition was seamless.

"Tell Sarah it is perfectly stable."

Orin made a sound that was not agreement.

The frame jack complained under her hands. The Anchor Maw Core burned white at its center. The surface of the bridge looked calm only from the side that did not have to hold it.

"Perfectly stable by your standards," Orin said.

"By my current standards," Rook answered.

Eli looked down.

The broken half of his staff had stopped clicking.

It lay beside his boot with its split side open to the air, dark where it should have held pale inlay. Then, very faintly, a thread of light moved through the hollow. Not enough to mend it. Not enough to use. Only enough to answer something far away.

The pulse came twice.

Once toward the voice in Rook's scanner.

Once toward the north route beyond the workshop wall.

Eli did not touch it.

Orin saw him not touching it.

Her eyes moved from the broken staff to the portal, then to Rook's wrist device. "Some things answer before they understand the question."

Eli swallowed whatever response had started in his throat.

"Yes," Eli said.

It was not agreement.

It was recognition.

Avery looked from the portal to Rook's wrong equipment. "Who are you really?"

Rook exhaled. For the first time, he looked tired—not physically, but existentially, like someone carrying a weight that had no measurable mass.

"My name is Rook." He said it like a statement of fact, not identity. "I'm part of a research team working on Project Chimera. We study dimensional anomalies. I was part of a transfer test, and something went wrong with the coordinates. I ended up here."

That was true.

It also left out the part that mattered.

Project Chimera was the breach name, not the whole machine. The people above it had sent teams toward stranger places before and filed the losses into cleaner words.

This place was different. It had not opened for a machine in a clean white room alone. It had needed an answering door here: stone, cord, chalk, old guilt, and an Anchor Maw Core seated by hands that had never heard the operation's name.

The thought left Rook colder than the portal had.

"The timing was off," he said quietly. "The matrix wasn't stabilizing properly. We were supposed to stagger the transitions—ten-second intervals. Avery Chen first, then Elijah, then Kaden, then Sarah, then me last. But the field flickered, and the spatial matrix started collapsing, and I had to go before it was ready."

Avery didn't speak.

"I stepped forward," Rook continued, "and for a fraction of a second, there were two of us in the same space. Two identical readings. Then one vanished. The other continued forward." He looked at his hands again. "Slightly altered. Slightly wrong."

Eli stepped forward. "Project Chimera."

Rook nodded once. "The project name is not the classified part."

"This is not your first door," Avery said.

"Not mine personally." Rook glanced at the frame, and something guarded passed over his face. "Not the first attempted breach. Not the first place the people above us have tried to touch. But this is the first time the team assigned to Project Chimera has made full contact with this world."

Orin's voice cut in from the frame jack. "Your people have been opening doors without knowing where they go?"

Rook looked at her.

For a moment, the old Continuum Rook's face seemed to show through the wrong one, wearing the expression of a man who understood exactly how badly a secret could rot inside useful work.

"Yes," Rook answered.

Orin tightened the jack another fraction. "That was the wrong answer."

"It usually is," Rook said.

"Who is above the project?" Avery asked.

Rook's expression closed with practiced speed.

"Classified," Rook answered.

Eli looked at him. "That is a very tidy word for refusing to say something."

"Yes," Rook said. "It is designed to be."

Avery kept his hand near the pack. "You're not from here."

"No." Rook met his gaze directly. "I'm from the place where the rules work the way most people expect them to. Where stone is just stone, and portals are theoretical unless someone has signed enough forms to be lied to properly, and people don't walk into doorways that decide whether to let them out."

Eli made a sound that was almost a laugh. "That sounds boring."

Rook's mouth twitched. "It is. Until it isn't."

The broken staff half by Eli's boot gave a sharp, warning click.

"It's the limiter," Avery said. "The frame was never finished. It doesn't have one."

Rook looked at him sharply. "A limiter. You mean a termination device?"

"We mean the third part of the Rule of Three." Avery moved toward the frame. "Frame. Conduit. Limiter. Without the limiter, the bridge can't hold. It's trying to complete itself, and it's failing."

Rook raised his scanner. "If I can boost the signal through Kaden's position in the void, we might be able to use the transference matrix as a temporary anchor. But it's going to require coordination."

"Then move," Orin said. "Standing here naming what has gone wrong will not repair it."

Near the portal, Rook could feel the world trying to remember him incorrectly.

The stable anomaly shimmered in the chamber ahead, its edges clear enough to trust and wrong enough not to. Around it, the stone bore the marks of old work: brace slots, chalk residue, anchor cuts, the practical grammar of people who had once built a route here and known they might need to flee through it later. The air carried the faint mineral scent of Veilglass and the colder metallic note of a frame being forced to behave as if it had a limiter.

Rook stood with Avery, Eli, and Sister Orin while Kaden's voice came and went through the scanner's static-ridden speaker like a thought that had learned how to speak.

"Sarah is moving," Kaden said, his voice thin, then suddenly near. "Dr. Avery Chen too. Elijah is still cut off, but his signal is moving."

"Moving where?" Rook asked.

"Toward the convergence point," Kaden said. "I think."

Eli dragged a hand through his hair and looked toward the dark route leading away from the portal chamber. "That isn't the same as safe."

"No," Avery said.

Sister Orin stood nearest the portal itself, one weathered hand resting lightly against the stone beside it rather than the anomaly directly. She watched it the way one watched fire in a dry season: not afraid of it exactly, but never trusting it to remember limits on its own.

"If the signals are converging," she said, "they will not converge at the same pace."

Rook barely heard her.

A pressure had started again behind his eyes.

Not pain. Not yet.

A flash of another corridor. A younger set of hands, gloveless, sliding a survey marker into a crack in the wall. A muttered curse at crooked stone. A laugh from farther ahead. Then the whole thing broke apart before he could hold it.

He steadied himself against a brace post.

Eli noticed immediately. "You all right?"

Rook exhaled once through his nose. "No, but I seem to be continuing anyway."

That earned the smallest possible snort from Eli.

Kaden's voice crackled back in. "Elijah's signal just shifted again. Faster."

Rook looked toward the tunnel leading east from the portal chamber before anyone else spoke.

He knew, with the same unreasonable certainty as the flashes, that if he stayed here waiting for the others to arrive neatly, something important would happen without him. Maybe dangerous. Maybe necessary. Maybe both.

"I'm going after him," he said.

That turned Avery, Eli, and Sister Orin toward him.

Avery answered first, because of course he did. "No."

"You don't know the route," Eli said over him.

"And you are least stable near thresholds," Sister Orin added, not unkindly.

Rook glanced at the portal, at the place that had already made a liar out of several laws he had once trusted. "Exactly."

Avery folded his arms. "The smart move is to stay put. Let the others come to the stable point."

Rook almost smiled.

"I was never very smart," he said. "I was just good at sounding like I had reasons."

Eli let out a rough laugh despite himself. Sister Orin did not smile, but something in her expression loosened by a fraction. Avery looked unimpressed in a way that suggested he might have liked Rook under different circumstances and resented that fact now.

"This is not wit," Avery said. "This is a bad decision."

"Probably," Rook agreed. "Still mine."

Another flash struck him before anyone could answer.

Stone under moon-cold light.

A voice saying his name, but not in Sarah's accent, not in Dr. Avery Chen's clipped precision, not in Kaden's void-thinned distance.

A sudden certainty that a leftward trail would lead to a ridge marked by three stakes and a broken limiter cap.

Rook shut his eyes against it.

When he opened them, Eli was watching him too closely.

"You're getting more of them," Eli said quietly.

Rook did not bother pretending otherwise. "Pieces."

"Whose?" Avery asked.

That was the question underneath everything, wasn't it.

Rook looked past them, into the tunnel that seemed to pull at some half-laid route in him. "I think," he said, and stopped because honesty sometimes arrived in fragments too, "I think they belong to the man who should have been here before me."

No one spoke for a moment.

Sister Orin broke the silence. "Then you must be careful not to mistake inheritance for instruction."

Rook looked at her. "That sounds like experience."

"It is age," she said. "Experience is the bruise age leaves."

Kaden came through the comm again, voice roughened by interference. "If Rook goes, he should not go alone."

The chamber quieted.

Then Avery made the decision the way practical people always did, by accepting the least-worse arrangement available.

"Fine," he said. "No one wanders blind. We split routes, hold voice contact when we can, and come back here if the signals collapse."

Eli straightened. "I'll take the north corridor. If Elijah's moving under old survey marks, I can read them faster than you can."

"I will stay with the portal," Sister Orin said. "And with Kaden's voice, if he can keep giving it to us. Sarah and Dr. Avery Chen will need a stable point when they arrive."

Avery nodded once. "Then I take west. Rook, east."

Eli looked at Rook again. "You sure you don't want somebody with you?"

Rook considered lying and decided it would waste everyone's time.

"No," he said. "But wanting sensible things has not improved the day so far."

That drew the ghost of a grin from Eli.

"Fair," Eli said.

Kaden's voice returned in a burst of static, thrown thinly through the scanner for all of them. "I can keep the channel open in pieces. Not continuously. The gaps are getting worse."

There was strain in him now, more obvious than before. Not physical breathlessness exactly, but the sound of someone holding too many distances together at once. Rook had heard enough lab reports in his life to know the sound of a system being used past its intended tolerance.

"Kaden," Rook said, quieter, "how hard is this hitting you?"

The reply took too long.

"I know where you all are," Kaden said at last. "That is the useful answer."

Not the real one.

Rook filed that away.

He had already gathered enough scattered hints to see the outline of something ugly: the company tests, the void adaptation, the way Kaden spoke about the space between worlds as if it were less a discovery than a scar that had learned to function. And beneath that, another implication still taking shape. A missing counterpart. An absence where there should have been a mirrored life. Not proof. Not yet. Just the shape of a question that nobody in this chamber was ready to say aloud.

Sister Orin seemed to hear the silence around that question all the same.

"What is made by force," she said, eyes still on the portal, "often arrives carrying an absence with it."

Kaden did not answer.

Neither did Rook.

Eli shifted his pack higher on one shoulder. Avery checked the axe at his belt. The chamber seemed to draw taut around each of them, reality narrowing itself into a set of chosen routes.

Rook looked once more into the eastern tunnel.

He did not know whether the pull he felt there belonged to Elijah, to the Rook who had been lost before him, or to the Continuum itself arranging pieces into a pattern it wanted finished.

It did not matter enough to wait.

"Voice check when you can," Avery said.

"If you hear screaming," Eli added, "try not to assume whose."

"Comforting," Rook said.

"I'm thoughtful that way."

Then they separated.

Eli vanished north into the older survey paths.

Avery moved west with the patient caution of someone who trusted procedure more than luck.

Sister Orin remained at the portal, one hand near stone, listening to Kaden's thinning voice and to the route itself.

Rook took the east tunnel alone.
